## 1. Purpose

This plan defines how AI ARK’s clickable prototype will be reviewed, tested, scored, remediated, and approved before concierge validation or engineering preparation.

It establishes:

- internal prototype review;
- participant recruitment;
- participant screening;
- research consent;
- moderated usability-test procedures;
- moderator scripts;
- task sequence;
- comprehension tests;
- behavioral measurements;
- confidence and trust measurements;
- creator and editor test variants;
- Mainland-specific test tasks;
- issue logging;
- issue-severity classification;
- evidence synthesis;
- design-remediation requirements;
- prototype decision gates;
- final GO / REMEDIATE / NO-GO criteria.

The plan is designed to test whether AI ARK’s differentiated concepts are understandable and valuable before production engineering begins.

---

## 2. Research Objective

# Determine whether target users can understand, trust, and successfully use the AI ARK product model through the clickable prototype.

The prototype should prove that users can:

- understand what AI ARK is;
- search for a capability by task;
- select a suitable Skill;
- understand why one Resource ranks above another;
- distinguish score from evidence confidence;
- understand what AI ARK Verification means;
- understand that verification is scoped and version-specific;
- distinguish creator identity from Resource Verification;
- distinguish verified usage from Resource Verification;
- understand Mainland availability;
- follow a Curated Workflow;
- distinguish Curated Workflows from AI-Generated Paths;
- contribute a structured review;
- understand creator claims and AI ARK Labs;
- understand the GitHub URL-to-publication editorial workflow.

---

## 3. Research Decisions Supported

The usability study should support decisions on:

1. whether AI ARK’s product proposition is clear;
2. whether task-oriented search is the correct homepage entry;
3. whether “capability” or “Skill” should lead public messaging;
4. whether Quality Score belongs above the fold;
5. whether Evidence Confidence is understandable;
6. whether Category Rank adds value;
7. whether verification levels are distinguishable;
8. whether trust, identity, usage-evidence, and commercial labels are visually distinct;
9. whether the Resource Detail page is appropriately structured;
10. whether the page is too long or information-dense;
11. whether Curated Workflows add value beyond Resource lists;
12. whether Mainland availability is understandable;
13. whether AI ARK Labs deserves primary navigation;
14. whether the review form captures enough evidence without excessive burden;
15. whether the editorial review workspace appears operationally credible;
16. whether the prototype is ready for concierge validation.

Optional.

---

## 12. Moderator Conduct

The moderator should:

- ask the participant to think aloud;
- avoid explaining features prematurely;
- avoid defending the design;
- ask what the participant expects before clicking;
- distinguish confusion from preference;
- allow productive struggle;
- intervene only after the pre-defined threshold.

The moderator should not say:

- “This is the verification badge.”
- “The answer is in the right panel.”
- “You should click the ranking.”
- “Most users understand this.”

## 37. Issue Severity Model

# Severity 0 — Critical

Definition:

The issue creates:

- dangerous misunderstanding;
- privacy or security risk;
- legal or compliance risk;
- inability to complete a core journey;
- false verification or availability interpretation.

Examples:

- users think a verified Resource is guaranteed safe;
- commercial badge appears equivalent to verification;
- prototype suggests automatic execution;
- Mainland feature appears to provide circumvention;
- private evidence appears publicly visible.

Required action:

> Must fix before further testing.

---

# Severity 1 — Major

Definition:

The issue causes:

- repeated task failure;
- major trust confusion;
- wrong Resource choice;
- incorrect version interpretation;
- inability to understand workflow status.

Required action:

> Must fix before prototype GO.

---

# Severity 2 — Moderate

Definition:

The issue creates:

- hesitation;
- inefficiency;
- secondary misunderstanding;
- excessive cognitive load;
- form friction.

Required action:

> Fix before concierge validation where practical.

---

# Severity 3 — Minor

Definition:

The issue is:

- cosmetic;
- wording;
- small alignment;
- low-impact preference.

Required action:

> Prioritize based on frequency and cost.

Do not explain the long-term vision before testing comprehension.

### 43.2 Avoid friendly-sample bias

Do not rely mainly on supporters.

### 43.3 Avoid confirmation coding

Record negative evidence equally.

### 43.4 Avoid prototype-beauty bias

Ask whether the product is useful, not only attractive.

### 43.5 Avoid leading trust language

Ask:

> What does this badge mean?

not:

> Do you understand that this is version-specific?

### 43.6 Avoid metric overconfidence

Small samples are directional.

Combine:

- behavior;
- comprehension;
- quotes;
- quantitative scores.

### 44.1 Immediate remediation

Fix before continuing sessions when:

- Severity 0 occurs;
- prototype link is broken;
- participant cannot distinguish fictional fixtures;
- a major task is technically impossible.

### 44.2 Batch remediation

After:

```text
5–7 sessions
```

review whether repeated issues justify a prototype iteration.

### 44.3 Versioning

Prototype iterations:

```text

v1.1
v1.2
```

Record:

- changed screens;
- issue IDs;
- date;
- expected effect.

### 44.4 Do not overreact

Do not redesign based on one low-severity preference.

## 46. Prototype Decision Gate

After external testing, choose one.

# GO — Concierge Validation Authorized

Requirements:

- no open Severity 0;
- no systemic Severity 1;
- comprehension thresholds substantially met;
- core discovery task succeeds;
- trust semantics understood;
- Resource pages add value;
- workflows show credible pull;
- prototype is stable enough for concierge use.

# GO WITH REMEDIATION

Requirements:

- core thesis supported;
- limited major issues remain;
- remediation is specific and low-risk;
- retest plan exists.

# REMEDIATE AND RETEST

Use when:

- core tasks fail;
- trust semantics are confused;
- Resource detail is unusable;
- workflows do not make sense;
- Mainland status is misunderstood;
- admin workflow is not credible.

# NO-GO / REPOSITION

Use when:

- users see no value beyond GitHub or existing directories;
- rankings reduce trust;
- workflows add no value;
- the product category remains unclear after remediation;
- the prototype reveals a fundamentally different product wedge.

## 61. Final Research Direction

# The prototype study should test the product’s meaning, not merely its visual appeal.

The key research question is not:

> Do users like the design?

It is:

> Can users use AI ARK to make a better capability decision, understand why that decision is supported, and recognize what remains uncertain?

The prototype is ready for concierge validation only when users can reliably understand:

```text
What AI ARK is
↓
What Resource they should use
↓
Why it ranks well
↓
What AI ARK verified
↓
Which version the evidence applies to
↓
Whether it works in their region
↓
How it fits into a workflow
↓
How to contribute evidence after use
```
