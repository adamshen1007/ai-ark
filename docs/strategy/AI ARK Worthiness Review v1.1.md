## 1. Executive Decision

# CONDITIONAL GO

AI ARK is worthy of proceeding into a tightly governed **Product Definition and Validation Phase**.

The project is not justified as:

- another AI tools navigation website;
- a catalogue whose main advantage is resource count;
- a copy of ColaSkill;
- a conventional transactional marketplace;
- a general AI news publication;
- a directory optimized mainly for GitHub stars;
- a FounderOS subsystem;
- a platform that automatically publishes or executes unreviewed third-party resources.

The project is justified as:

> **A trusted AI capability discovery, evidence, launch, testing, verification, workflow, and distribution platform for humans and AI agents.**

AI ARK should begin with **Skills** as the market-entry category and expand through a common resource model to:

- MCP servers;
- Agents;
- Plugins;
- Workflows;
- developer tools;
- other reusable AI capabilities.

Its long-term value is not the number of indexed resources. Its value is the **Capability Evidence Graph** connecting resources, creators, versions, sources, compatibility, tests, user outcomes, rankings, verification, regional availability, tasks, and workflows.

The project’s recommended progression is:

```text
Curated Skill Discovery
        ↓
Capability Evidence and Trust
        ↓
Creator Launch and Testing Network
        ↓
Task-to-Workflow Intelligence
        ↓
Human and Agent Discovery Infrastructure
```

### Updated overall score

**8.1 / 10 — Worth validating under strict scope and governance.**

This is an improvement over the previous review because the product now has a clearer data flywheel:

```text
Creators publish
        ↓
Users discover and test
        ↓
AI ARK gathers structured evidence
        ↓
Rankings and verification improve
        ↓
Workflows and recommendations improve
        ↓
More humans and agents use AI ARK
        ↓
More creators choose AI ARK
```

However, the additional product depth also creates substantially greater operational, compliance, moderation, and trust obligations. It increases the opportunity and the execution burden at the same time.

## 2. Product Definition

### 2.1 Category

The strongest category definition is:

# AI Capability Discovery and Intelligence Platform

A more complete internal definition is:

> **AI ARK helps humans and AI systems discover, understand, evaluate, test, verify, combine, access, and monitor the right AI capabilities for a specific task.**

### 2.2 Initial public proposition

At launch, the proposition should remain focused:

> **Find the right AI Skill for the job.**

Possible supporting line:

> Curated, explained, tested, and ranked for the way you work.

After expansion:

> Skills, MCPs, Agents, and workflows worth using.

### 2.3 Product philosophy

The original distribution analogy remains useful:

> We do not need to produce every AI capability. We discover, organize, verify, connect, and deliver the capabilities worth using.

This should not imply that AI ARK bears no responsibility for what it distributes. Once AI ARK ranks, verifies, mirrors, recommends, or includes a resource inside a workflow, it takes on a meaningful trust obligation.

### 2.4 Core user decisions

Every AI ARK resource page should help a user answer:

1. **Can this solve my problem?**
2. **Is it suitable for my environment?**
3. **Why should I trust the information?**
4. **What evidence exists that it works?**
5. **How do I install, deploy, or use it?**
6. **What are the risks and limitations?**
7. **What should I use instead if it is unavailable or unsuitable?**
8. **How does it fit into a larger workflow?**

## 3. Problem Worth Solving

### 3.1 Supply fragmentation

AI capabilities are distributed across:

- GitHub repositories;
- product websites;
- documentation sites;
- package registries;
- MCP registries;
- official agent directories;
- X;
- Reddit;
- Hacker News;
- Product Hunt;
- newsletters;
- creator communities;
- Chinese technical platforms;
- WeChat articles;
- Bilibili;
- Xiaohongshu;
- private communities.

These sources are optimized for code hosting, publishing, conversation, or promotion. They are generally not optimized for comparative capability selection.

### 3.2 Decision friction

A user often needs to investigate:

- what the resource actually does;
- which task it solves;
- which versions are current;
- whether it is actively maintained;
- whether the creator is genuine;
- which agent or environment supports it;
- whether compatibility is explicit, inferred, or tested;
- what installation requires;
- which permissions and dependencies are involved;
- whether it is available in the user’s region;
- whether other users obtained real results;
- whether alternatives are better;
- whether the information is still current.

This creates a large gap between **finding a link** and **making a confident decision**.

### 3.3 Agent-side discovery problem

AI agents face the same issue in structured form. They need reliable answers to questions such as:

- What capabilities exist?
- Which are compatible with this runtime?
- Which version should be used?
- Is the resource verified?
- Is it available in this region?
- What permissions does it require?
- What evidence supports its ranking?
- Which workflow step does it satisfy?
- What alternative exists if it fails?

Most existing directories are built primarily for human browsing and return insufficiently governed information for machine consumption.

### 3.4 Creator distribution problem

Creators need:

- understandable presentation;
- launch visibility;
- creator identity;
- beta testers;
- structured feedback;
- international or Chinese localization;
- usage evidence;
- referral analytics;
- an authorized canonical page;
- recognition for quality and adoption.

GitHub alone does not provide all of these.

### Problem decision

# GO

The problem is real, growing, and valuable enough to validate.

## 4. Market Timing

### 4.1 Positive signals

The timing is favorable because:

- Agent Skills have an open and structured format centered on `SKILL.md`.
- Skills and MCP solve complementary capability needs.
- Official platforms are unifying discovery of Skills, Connectors, and Plugins.
- The official MCP Registry provides a structured distribution and ownership-verification surface.
- More creators are publishing portable capabilities.
- Security and dependency risks are receiving increasing attention.
- Human users and agents both need better capability selection.
- Chinese and global ecosystems remain fragmented from each other.

Anthropic’s unified Skills, Connectors, and Plugins directory validates the convergence of multiple capability types into one discovery surface. The open Agent Skills specification also supports the possibility of cross-platform portability rather than permanent dependence on one vendor.

### 4.2 Negative signals

The market is no longer empty.

AI ARK will compete with:

- official platform directories;
- MCP registries;
- large community Skill catalogues;
- Chinese-language directories;
- GitHub-native discovery;
- general AI tool directories;
- search engines and AI answer engines;
- existing creator communities.

A basic directory launched now would have weak differentiation.

### 4.3 Timing conclusion

> **The directory layer is already crowded. The evidence, trust, workflow, and cross-ecosystem intelligence layer remains open enough to justify validation.**

### Timing decision

# CONDITIONAL GO

The product must enter quickly enough to learn from the market but slowly enough to establish trustworthy foundations.

## 8. Jobs to Be Done

### Human discovery job

> When I need AI to perform a particular task, help me find and understand the best available capability without researching many disconnected sources.

### Workflow job

> When my objective requires multiple capabilities, give me a practical sequence that explains what to use, in what order, and what output to expect.

### Creator launch job

> When I release or test a capability, give me a trusted launch page, relevant testers, useful feedback, and distribution to the right users.

### Creator improvement job

> Show me where users fail, which environments work, what issues repeat, and how my resource compares with alternatives.

### Trust job

> Help me distinguish popular, high-quality, well-maintained, safe-enough, and broadly proven capabilities without hiding uncertainty.

### Agent job

> Return structured, current, evidence-backed capability candidates that match task, environment, region, cost, and risk requirements.

### Editorial job

> Convert a source URL into a high-quality publication draft quickly while preserving factual traceability and human approval.

## 10. Ranking System

### 10.1 Decision

# STRONG GO

Ranking is central to discovery, but the platform must not claim philosophical objectivity.

The defensible promise is:

> **Transparent, evidence-based, reproducible, category-normalized ranking with disclosed uncertainty.**

### 10.2 Separate score types

AI ARK should expose:

#### Quality Score

How effective, reliable, documented, and maintainable the resource appears to be.

#### Category Rank

Position among comparable resources in the same category and lifecycle class.

#### Momentum Score

Recent adoption, update activity, testing, and discussion growth.

#### Confidence Score

Strength and quantity of reliable evidence behind the evaluation.

#### Risk Status

Security, dependency, permission, maintenance, and integrity concerns.

Example:

```text
AI ARK Quality Score       88/100
Presentation Skills Rank   #2
Momentum                   Rising
Evidence Confidence        High
Risk Status                Review complete; not security-audited
```

### 10.3 Proposed stable-resource dimensions

| Dimension | Initial weight |
|---|---:|
| Functional effectiveness | 25% |
| Verified user outcomes | 15% |
| Reliability and reproducibility | 15% |
| Maintenance and freshness | 10% |
| Documentation and onboarding | 10% |
| Compatibility breadth | 10% |
| Verified adoption | 10% |
| Creator responsiveness | 5% |

The weights are hypotheses and must be calibrated through validation.

### 10.4 Security treatment

Security should act as:

- an eligibility gate;
- a penalty;
- a warning;
- a possible disqualification.

Popularity must not outweigh a serious unresolved security risk.

### 10.5 Category and lifecycle fairness

Beta resources should not compete directly with established production resources.

Separate ranking views should include:

- Best Verified;
- Most Adopted;
- Rising;
- New and Promising;
- Beta Testing;
- Editor’s Picks.

### 10.6 Ranking integrity

Required controls:

- logarithmic treatment of stars and downloads;
- Bayesian smoothing;
- minimum sample thresholds;
- freshness decay;
- category normalization;
- version-specific aggregation;
- duplicate and fork handling;
- suspicious-review detection;
- verified-use weighting;
- transparent manual intervention logs;
- public methodology;
- appeals.

### 10.7 Commercial independence

These must never influence organic rank:

- sponsorship;
- paid placement;
- creator subscription;
- exclusive launch;
- evaluation fee;
- strategic partnership.

Commercial content must be clearly identified and separated.

### 10.8 Mainland China consideration

AI ARK’s ranking, search, selection, and recommendation functions may fall within China’s algorithm-recommendation governance scope. The Mainland product therefore requires published rules, documented governance, human intervention mechanisms, user-choice controls where applicable, and specialist compliance review before launch.

## 13. AI ARK Verification

### 13.1 Decision

# STRONG GO WITH VERSIONED, LAYERED GOVERNANCE

Verification should never be a single ambiguous badge.

### 13.2 Verification ladder

#### Level 1 — Source Verified

Confirms:

- canonical source;
- creator or organization identity;
- ownership or authorization;
- license;
- current version.

#### Level 2 — Functionally Tested

Confirms:

- installation was completed;
- defined scenarios were executed;
- core claims were reproduced;
- environment and version are disclosed.

#### Level 3 — Quality Verified

Confirms:

- quality threshold passed;
- documentation met minimum standards;
- reliability criteria were met;
- no unresolved disqualifying issue was found within scope;
- evidence is available.

#### Level 4 — Proven in Use

Confirms:

- sufficient verified adoption;
- meaningful user outcomes;
- acceptable failure profile;
- repeat-use or production evidence where applicable.

#### Top designation — AI ARK Verified

Requires:

```text
Source Verified
+
Functionally Tested
+
Quality Verified
+
Adequate real-use evidence
```

### 13.3 Niche-resource fairness

A niche tool may be excellent without broad adoption.

Therefore:

- Quality Verified measures excellence;
- Proven in Use measures real-world adoption;
- AI ARK Verified may require both.

### 13.4 Verification report

Every result should state:

- version;
- evaluation date;
- expiry date;
- test environments;
- scenarios;
- evidence reviewed;
- limitations;
- unresolved risks;
- evaluator;
- conflicts of interest;
- appeal process.

### 13.5 Re-review triggers

- major release;
- ownership change;
- dependency change;
- permission change;
- serious issue;
- maintenance lapse;
- verification expiry;
- regional-delivery change.

### 13.6 Payment policy

A future evaluation fee may purchase the evaluation process.

It must never purchase a successful badge.

## 31. Validation Metrics

### Gate A — Discovery

After at least 1,000 qualified sessions:

- search/category result → resource page: **≥30%**;
- resource page → source/install action: **≥15%**;
- immediate search reformulation: **<35%**;
- at least 20 users confirm meaningful research-time savings.

### Gate B — Workflow value

- at least 60% of tested users rate a curated workflow useful;
- at least 20% open or save two or more workflow resources;
- at least 10 users complete a meaningful workflow step;
- generated paths are clearly distinguished from verified workflows.

### Gate C — Editorial economics

Across at least 100 imports:

- median URL-to-draft: **≤2 minutes**;
- median human review: **≤5 minutes**;
- material post-publication factual correction: **<5%**;
- factual-field source coverage: **100%**;
- duplicate-detection precision: **≥95%** in test corpus.

### Gate D — Creator value

- at least five creators claim a page;
- at least two run or commit to a beta campaign;
- at least five share their AI ARK page;
- at least two express interest in AI ARK First, Exclusive Beta, or Official Page.

### Gate E — Community evidence

- at least 25 structured reviews;
- at least ten verified-use evidence submissions;
- at least five creator responses;
- no unresolved severe moderation failure.

### Gate F — Agent value

- FounderOS or a test client can consume the read API;
- at least 20 successful structured discovery requests;
- all machine responses include version, provenance, and freshness;
- API never implies installation authorization.

### Gate G — Commercial signal

Obtain at least one:

- three paid launch or placement pilots;
- two credible API/enterprise design partners;
- ten creators willing to pay for analytics or distribution;
- one meaningful platform or community partnership.

Stop or substantially reposition if two or more occur:

1. source/install action remains below 10%;
2. users consistently prefer GitHub or official directories after seeing AI ARK;
3. returning usage remains below 8%;
4. workflow pages show no improvement over resource listings;
5. creators do not claim or share pages;

8. ranking is not trusted by users or creators;
9. Labs requires excessive founder-managed recruitment;
10. moderation becomes disproportionate to usage;
