## 2. Executive Product Definition

# AI ARK is the trusted discovery and intelligence layer for AI capabilities.

AI ARK helps humans and AI systems:

- discover;
- understand;
- compare;
- test;
- rank;
- verify;
- combine;
- access;
- monitor;

the right AI capabilities for a specific task.

The platform begins with **AI Skills** and expands through a common resource model to include:

- Skills;
- MCP servers;
- Agents;
- Plugins;
- Workflows;
- developer tools;
- other reusable AI capabilities.

AI ARK does not need to create every capability it distributes.

Its role is to:

> **Find, organize, evaluate, verify, connect, and deliver the capabilities worth using.**

# Help every human and AI agent find the right capability for the job.

AI ARK exists to reduce the effort, uncertainty, and risk involved in selecting and using external AI capabilities.

- an individual developer looking for a useful Skill;
- a founder assembling an AI-enabled workflow;
- a creator seeking early testers;

## 4. Long-Term Vision

# Become the capability map and trust infrastructure for the AI agent ecosystem.

In the long term, AI ARK should be able to answer:

- What AI capabilities exist?
- Which capability is best for this task?
- Which versions are active and maintained?
- Which environments support them?
- What evidence confirms they work?
- What risks or limitations are known?
- Are they available in the user’s region?
- Which alternatives should be considered?
- Which sequence of capabilities can complete the user’s objective?
- Which capabilities can an AI agent safely consider for execution planning?

Agents
  ↓
AI ARK MCP and Connectors

## 5. The Market Problem

### 5.1 Capability supply is expanding faster than discoverability

New Skills, MCP servers, Agents, Plugins, and workflows are published continuously across:

- GitHub;
- official platform directories;
- product websites;
- package registries;
- MCP registries;
- X;
- Reddit;
- Hacker News;
- Product Hunt;
- newsletters;
- technical communities;
- Chinese social and developer platforms.

The supply is fragmented across ecosystems, languages, regions, and distribution channels.

### 5.2 Existing sources answer the wrong questions

GitHub answers:

> What did the developer publish?

Social platforms answer:

> What is receiving attention now?

Official directories answer:

> What works inside this platform?

General AI directories answer:

> What AI products exist?

Users need answers to a different set of questions:

> What should I use for my task, why should I trust it, how do I use it, and what should I expect?

### 5.3 Resource count creates noise rather than confidence

A directory with thousands of entries can make discovery harder when it lacks:

- clear categorization;
- version awareness;
- objective comparison;
- evidence;
- freshness;
- compatibility;
- user outcomes;
- regional availability;
- workflow context.

The problem is no longer access to links.

The problem is **decision quality**.

### 5.4 AI agents face the same problem

AI agents require structured information that ordinary directories rarely provide:

- canonical identity;
- version;
- compatibility;
- installation requirements;
- evidence;
- verification;
- risk status;
- regional availability;
- alternatives;
- workflow position;
- freshness.

Without this information, agent-side discovery is unreliable.

### 5.5 Creators lack a complete launch and evidence system

Creators need more than repository hosting.

They need:

- high-quality presentation;
- creator identity;
- distribution;
- beta testers;
- structured feedback;
- launch analytics;
- international localization;
- Chinese localization;
- verified usage evidence;
- a canonical publication page;
- a credible route toward quality recognition.

AI ARK can serve both the demand side and the creator side without becoming a transactional marketplace during the initial stage.

## 7. Core Product Thesis

AI ARK is based on seven strategic hypotheses.

### Thesis 1 — Discovery is becoming the bottleneck

The AI ecosystem is producing more capabilities than users can reliably evaluate.

### Thesis 2 — Trust is more valuable than catalogue size

Users will return to the source that helps them make confident decisions, not necessarily the source with the most entries.

### Thesis 3 — Evidence can become a defensible asset

Source provenance, testing results, verified usage, compatibility history, and version-specific reviews accumulate over time.

### Thesis 4 — Outcomes are more important than resource names

Users search for goals such as:

> Build a presentation, research a market, test a product, deploy a service.

They do not always know which Skill, MCP, or Agent they need.

### Thesis 5 — Creators need launch and testing infrastructure

Creators can become AI ARK’s supply and distribution partners when AI ARK provides meaningful launch, testing, feedback, and localization value.

### Thesis 6 — Humans and agents need the same underlying intelligence

The interfaces differ, but both need structured, current, explainable capability information.

### Thesis 7 — China and global ecosystems require a trusted bridge

AI ARK can create value by:

- discovering global resources;
- explaining them in Chinese;
- clarifying Mainland availability;
- identifying lawful delivery options;
- introducing Chinese creators to international users;
- preserving provenance in both directions.

## 10. Jobs to Be Done

### 10.1 Discovery job

> When I need an AI capability, help me find the best realistic options without researching many disconnected sources.

### 10.2 Decision job

> Help me determine whether a capability is suitable, maintained, compatible, and trustworthy enough to try.

### 10.3 Onboarding job

> Show me how to install and use the capability, including useful first prompts and expected outputs.

### 10.4 Workflow job

> When one capability is insufficient, give me an ordered path that combines multiple capabilities to complete my goal.

### 10.5 Regional-access job

> Tell me whether the capability is available and operational in my region, and provide lawful alternatives when it is not.

### 10.6 Creator-launch job

> Help me present and launch my capability to the right users without building a separate distribution operation.

### 10.7 Beta-testing job

> Help me recruit suitable testers, collect structured feedback, and improve my resource before broad release.

### 10.8 Verification job

> Give users credible evidence that my capability is authentic, functional, high quality, and proven within a clearly defined scope.

### 10.9 Agent-discovery job

> Return structured capability candidates with enough evidence for planning and recommendation.

## 12. Product Experience Principles

### Principle 1 — Outcome before implementation

Explain what the capability helps the user accomplish before exposing repository or technical detail.

### Principle 2 — Evidence before confidence

AI ARK should not make strong claims without showing why they are believed.

### Principle 3 — Curation before scale

A smaller trusted collection is more valuable than a large undifferentiated catalogue.

### Principle 4 — Human review before publication

AI may prepare drafts, but publication and material verification require governed human decisions.

### Principle 5 — Version before generalization

Compatibility, testing, ranking, and reviews should bind to specific versions.

### Principle 6 — Task before taxonomy

Categories help browsing, but user goals should drive recommendations and workflows.

### Principle 7 — Neutrality before ecosystem preference

AI ARK should not favor a platform because of undisclosed commercial relationships.

### Principle 8 — Transparency before authority

Ranking and verification should disclose methods, scope, evidence, and uncertainty.

### Principle 9 — Regional truth before false availability

AI ARK should clearly distinguish information access, artifact access, installation access, and verified runtime access.

### Principle 10 — Discovery before execution

Initial AI ARK interfaces should recommend and inform. They should not automatically execute third-party resources.

### Principle 11 — Creator value without purchased credibility

Creators may pay for distribution or evaluation processes, but not for organic rank or successful verification.

### Principle 12 — Original identity, benchmark-quality presentation

AI ARK may learn from ColaSkill’s editorial quality while maintaining its own visual identity and stronger trust system.

## 14. Ranking Positioning

AI ARK ranking should not be marketed as absolute truth.

The public promise should be:

> **Transparent, evidence-based rankings within clearly defined categories and lifecycle stages.**

AI ARK should separate:

- Quality Score;
- Category Rank;
- Momentum;
- Evidence Confidence;
- Risk Status.

The ranking system should communicate:

- why the resource ranked;
- which evidence was used;
- which version was evaluated;
- how current the result is;
- what uncertainty remains.

Sponsored placements, creator subscriptions, exclusive launches, and paid evaluation must not influence organic rankings.

---

## 15. Verification Positioning

Verification should represent evidence, not marketing.

Recommended hierarchy:

1. Source Verified  
2. Functionally Tested  
3. Quality Verified  
4. Proven in Use  
5. AI ARK Verified  

The public meaning of AI ARK Verified should be:

> AI ARK confirmed the canonical source, reproduced defined core functionality, evaluated quality within a published scope, and reviewed sufficient real-use evidence for the stated version.

It should not mean:

- perfectly secure;
- guaranteed to work everywhere;
- officially endorsed by every supported platform;
- permanently approved;
- free from future risk.

Every verification should be:

- versioned;
- dated;
- scoped;
- evidence-backed;
- appealable;
- subject to expiry and re-review.

## 18. Workflow Positioning

AI ARK should distinguish between finding a resource and solving a problem.

The workflow proposition is:

> **Describe your goal. AI ARK shows the capabilities and path required to accomplish it.**

Two workflow classes should be visible:

### AI ARK Verified Workflow

- designed or reviewed by humans;
- tested end to end;
- versioned;
- evidence-backed;
- maintained.

### AI-Generated Path

- generated dynamically;
- adapted to constraints;
- clearly labelled;
- not necessarily tested end to end.

This distinction protects trust while enabling scalable personalization.

## 19. China and Global Positioning

AI ARK has a strategic opportunity to become a two-way bridge.

### 19.1 Global to China

AI ARK can:

- discover high-quality global resources;
- explain them in Chinese;
- clarify access and installation conditions;
- coordinate authorized localization;
- identify lawful mirrors or creator-provided editions;
- recommend domestic alternatives;
- verify Mainland runtime where possible.

### 19.2 China to Global

AI ARK can:

- identify high-quality Chinese creators;
- produce English-language pages;
- clarify source and ownership;
- provide global distribution;
- include Chinese capabilities in international workflows;
- help creators attract testers and users outside China.

### 19.3 Regional-availability promise

AI ARK should never reduce availability to a binary yes/no state.

It should distinguish:

- information accessible;
- artifact accessible;
- installation accessible;
- runtime verified.

### 19.4 Compliance posture

AI ARK should use:

- lawful hosting;
- rights-based mirroring;
- explicit creator authorization;
- transparent source attribution;
- regional data design;
- specialist legal and regulatory review.

AI ARK should not provide access-control circumvention.

## 28. Strategic Product Boundaries

AI ARK should not become the following during the initial product phase.

### Not a generic AI tools directory

The scope centers on reusable capabilities and practical workflows.

### Not a transactional marketplace

Payments, commissions, and seller economics are deferred.

### Not a general social network

Community functionality exists to produce useful evidence and creator feedback.

### Not a general AI news publication

News supports resource discovery and change detection.

### Not a model or agent vendor

AI ARK remains neutral across ecosystems.

### Not an automatic execution platform

Initial interfaces support discovery and planning only.

### Not a security guarantee

Verification is scoped and evidence-based.

### Not a popularity leaderboard

Ranking includes effectiveness, reliability, maintenance, evidence, and risk.

### Not a Mainland access circumvention service

Availability must be lawful, transparent, and rights-based.

### Not a FounderOS module

AI ARK remains independently deployable and accessible through a connector.

## 32. Success Definition

AI ARK succeeds when users intentionally return because it helps them make better capability decisions.

### Early success

- users find suitable resources faster;
- resource pages produce meaningful source/install actions;
- creators claim and share pages;
- ingestion drafts require limited correction;
- workflows are judged more useful than isolated listings;
- users contribute structured evidence.

### Medium-term success

- AI ARK ranking is trusted;
- AI ARK Verification influences adoption;
- creators choose AI ARK for launches and beta tests;
- regional availability becomes a recognised advantage;
- agents consume AI ARK through APIs or connectors.

### Long-term success

- AI ARK becomes a default capability-discovery layer;
- enterprise teams use AI ARK for governance;
- AI agents rely on AI ARK for capability planning;
- the Capability Evidence Graph becomes a strategic data asset.

## 33. North Star Metric

The recommended North Star concept is:

# Successful Capability Discoveries

A successful capability discovery occurs when a user or agent:

1. expresses a task or browsing intent;
2. evaluates a relevant resource or workflow;
3. takes a meaningful next action.

Meaningful actions may include:

- opening the canonical source;
- copying an installation command;
- saving the resource;
- adding it to a workflow;
- applying to test it;
- installing or deploying it;
- requesting it through the API;
- selecting an alternative;
- returning to submit evidence.

Supporting metrics:

- search-to-resource conversion;
- resource-to-source/install conversion;
- workflow completion;
- return rate;
- creator claim rate;
- verified usage;
- agent API success;
- editorial cost;
- evidence coverage.
